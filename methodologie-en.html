Methodology and sources

This page documents, in a single place and with no JavaScript required, the method and sources of the historical globe of Armenia (−10,000 → 2026, 88 time steps).

World borders

The historical-basemaps project (A. Ourednik), community-driven and neutral — no single governmental source, no territorial bias.

Nagorno-Karabakh outline

Polygon of the historical NKAO (1923–1991) reconstructed from neutral sources. No Azerbaijani source was used for this outline.

Armenian settlement layer

A graded layer shows the areas of Armenian settlement independently of political borders. It is an indicative reconstruction by broad eras, built on the provinces of the Ashkharhatsuyts and validated by an independent historian-demographer audit. The percentages are contested ranges depending on the sources; these territories have always been multiethnic, and the layer concerns only the Armenian component. It describes a historical settlement and implies no current territorial claim.

Lakes, seas, rivers and relief

Lakes and inland seas (Caspian, Black Sea): Natural Earth (scale 1:50m), redrawn on top of the territories to remain visible in every period. Major rivers (Euphrates, Tigris, Araxes, Kura, Nile, Indus, Ganges, Yellow River, Danube): simplified outlines drawn by hand over established geographic landmarks. Relief (Greater Caucasus range): a stylised ridge line anchored on real summits, without full hillshading.

Historical notices

Summaries written for this project, verified by independent historian agents, supplemented with Wikipedia summaries on request. No data is invented: every reconstruction is flagged as such in the interface.

Anachronism corrections

The base cartographic source contained several anachronisms, corrected with sourcing (never by assumption) — for example: the kingdom of Urartu named “Urartu / Ararat” and not anticipated before its founding around 860 BC; the Hattian city-states (and not “Hittites”) at −2000; the Sultanate of Rum distinguished from the Anatolian beyliks and then from Ottoman Anatolia (1279→1400); Safavid Yerevan in 1700 (and not Ottoman). Each correction is recorded in an internal audit register.

Geometric precision

The outlines are at atlas scale (simplified): reliable for locating an entity, but unfit to settle a border dispute down to the kilometre.

Bibliography
