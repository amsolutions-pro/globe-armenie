Syria (France)

Year on the map : 1923

This outline groups under a single “France” label two genuinely distinct administrations of the immediate post-World War I period: the coastal zone occupied by France (OETA West) and Emir Faisal's Arab administration (OETA East, the interior including Damascus), proclaimed the Kingdom of Syria on 7 March 1920 before being destroyed by the French army at the Battle of Maysalun (24-25 July 1920). The San Remo conference (April 1920) formally assigned the Syrian mandate to France, but its effective authority over the interior — Damascus included, within this outline — only truly began after Maysalun: for the earliest occurrences in this dataset, the “France” label may therefore run ahead of the situation on the ground by several months to almost two years. This division roughly followed the administrative units inherited from the Ottoman Empire. Notably, the Bekaa plain (the kazas of Baalbek, Hasbaya, and Rachaya), despite lying between Mount Lebanon and the Anti-Lebanon range, remained under the Arab OETA East rather than the French OETA West — with the sole exception of the town of Zahlé, administered by France from the outset. These districts were only detached from Syria upon the proclamation of the State of Greater Lebanon on 1 September 1920, following the collapse of the Arab Kingdom of Syria at the Battle of Maysalun. A precise cartographic split between the two administrations is not modelled separately here, for lack of a canonical cartographic source for this short-lived internal border.
