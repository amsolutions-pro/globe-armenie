Poland-Lithuania

Year on the map : 1500

Only this dataset's 1375 occurrence is anachronistic: at that date, Poland (Casimir III, then Louis I of Hungary-Poland) and Lithuania (Grand Duke Algirdas, expanding massively into the eastern Rus' principalities) remained two fully sovereign kingdoms with no dynastic link — the personal union that would create Poland-Lithuania only began with the Union of Krewo (14 August 1385) and was completed by Jogaila's baptism, marriage and coronation as King of Poland in February-March 1386. The 1375 outline combines the two realms' extent under this single name for convenience, ahead of when it actually became one — a precise internal border between the two at that date is not yet modelled separately here. This same 1375 outline also wrongly includes Galicia (Halych, Lwów/Lviv, Przemyśl) within the “Poland-Lithuania” block: the region belonged at the time neither to Poland nor to Lithuania, but was administered directly by the Kingdom of Hungary — whose king, Louis I of Anjou, had also been King of Poland since 1370 (Hungarian-Polish personal union, 1370-1382) — through his governor Ladislaus of Opole (1372-1378), before Galicia's formal attachment to the Hungarian crown in 1378. As with the Polish-Lithuanian border itself, no reliable line can be drawn to isolate this area at that date, so only this textual clarification is added, with no change to the geometry. From 1400 onward in this dataset, however, the union genuinely existed (personal union since 1386, tightened by the Union of Lublin in 1569) and the single label is accurate again.
