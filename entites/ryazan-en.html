Ryazan

Year on the map : 1492

In this dataset's 1279 and 1300 entries, the label “Ryazan” simplifies a vast expanse of Rus' lands fragmented by the Mongol invasion (1237-1240), all made tributary to the Golden Horde while keeping their local princes — but the actual Principality of Ryazan (on the Oka, sacked as early as December 1237) is only a small part of it, in the east. To the west, the lands stemming from the Kingdom of Rus' (crowned in the person of Daniel I in 1253) remained, at both dates, split into two distinct principalities, each retaining local authority despite paying the same Mongol tribute: Galicia, ruled by his son Leo I (r. 1264-1301); and Volhynia, ruled until 1289 by his cousin Volodymyr Vasylkovych (r. 1269-1289) and, from that date on, by his brother Mstyslav Danylovych, still in office in 1300. Galicia and Volhynia would only be united under a single prince in 1301 — on Leo I's death, by his son Yurii (George) I — that is, after both dates covered by this dataset; the two principalities are therefore likewise not modeled separately here. From 1492 onward in this dataset, however, the outline reverts to a genuinely independent and much smaller Principality of Ryazan, consistent with the historical reality of that later period (annexed by Moscow in 1521) — this notice only applies to the 1279 and 1300 occurrences.
