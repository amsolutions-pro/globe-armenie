Aghlabid Emirate

Year on the map : 885

The Aghlabid Emirate (800-909), nominally an Abbasid vassal ruling from Kairouan, controlled most of Ifriqiya (present-day Tunisia) and launched the conquest of Byzantine Sicily in 827. The simplified outline shown here covers the whole central Maghreb for convenience, but two distinct neighbouring states within it were genuinely independent, not Aghlabid: to the west, the Idrisid Emirate (Morocco, from 788, a sharifian dynasty at Fez); centrally, the Rustamid Imamate (Algeria, from 776-779, capital Tahert/Tiaret, an Ibadi dynasty) — the exact borders of the Idrisid state remain debated in the sources and are not yet modeled separately here. Those of the Rustamid imamate are of a different order: according to M. Talbi (art. “Rustamids,” Encyclopaedia of Islam, 2nd ed.), the authority of the Ibadi imam of Tahert had fluid frontiers, more human than geographical, defined by tribal allegiance rather than a fixed political line — making any single geometry structurally impossible to draw with precision. The territory actually controlled also contracted sharply between 800 (close to its apogee) and 900 (reduced to Tahert and its immediate surroundings from the 880s onward, well before the fall of 909) — two states too different to share a common boundary.
