Buwayhid Emirates

Year on the map : 1198

The Buyid emirate genuinely ruled from 945 until its fall in December 1055, when the Seljuk sultan Toghrul Beg took Baghdad. The upstream source, however, reuses the same outline without updating it well past that date (up to 1198/1239 in this dataset, i.e. 143-184 years after the dynasty's real end): in those later years, the label no longer reflects any Buyid political reality. The exact successor there remains uncertain without further research: Iraq itself then came for the first time under the direct temporal authority of the Abbasid caliph al-Nasir (r. 1180-1225), while western Iran (the Hamadan region) passed under Khwarezmian control after the defeat and death of the last Seljuk sultan, Toghrul III, near Rayy in 1194, and the Zangid atabegs held Mosul — three distinct powers that a single outline cannot faithfully represent without a precise cartographic source for an internal split. As of the precise date of 1239, the description above is itself already outdated in two respects: Mosul had passed from direct Zangid authority to the atabeg Badr al-Din Lu'lu' (regent since 1211, ruling in his own name after the death of the last Zangid ruler in 1233-1234), and western Iran no longer had a genuinely functional Khwarezmian state: the last Khwarezmshah, Jalal al-Din Mingburnu, had died in 1231, and the region had come under direct Mongol military occupation (General Chormaqan, from 1230-1231 onward; Isfahan taken in 1235-1236). No single cartographic source combines these elements into a precise composite boundary; this is therefore only a textual clarification, without new geometry.
