Phrygians

Year on the map : -705

Indo-European people (an isolated branch, closer to Greek than to Armenian) settled in central Anatolia after the collapse of the Hittite world (~1200 BC), with its capital at Gordion and the legendary figures of Gordias and King Midas of the golden touch. The kingdom peaked in the 8th century BC before being ravaged by the Cimmerian invasion (c. 696-675 BC, Midas is said by tradition, per Strabo, to have killed himself), then absorbed into the Lydian orbit. Herodotus (7.73) states that the Armenians were "equipped like the Phrygians, being Phrygian colonists" — an ancient tradition linking the two peoples to the Bryges/Mushki migration from the Balkans (see "Mushki", the eastern branch of the same migration, on the Armenian plateau). Modern linguists largely reject a close kinship: Phrygian, a centum language, is closer to Greek than to Armenian, a satem language — a possible contribution to Armenian ethnogenesis, not a direct origin.
